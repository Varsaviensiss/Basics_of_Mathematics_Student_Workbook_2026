# Exercise 1. Determinant of a 2×2 Matrix

For a \(2\times2\) matrix

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
$$

the determinant is calculated using the formula

$$
\det(A)=ad-bc
$$

## 1. Calculate the First Determinant

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&4\end{pmatrix}
$$

Using the formula:

$$
\det(A)=(3)(4)-(-2)(5)
$$

$$
=12-(-10)
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(A)=22}
$$

Since the determinant is not zero, the matrix \(A\) is invertible.

## 2. Calculate the Second Determinant

$$
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\2&14\end{pmatrix}
$$

Using the formula:

$$
\det(B)=(1)(14)-(7)(2)
$$

$$
=14-14
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(B)=0}
$$

Since the determinant is zero, the matrix \(B\) is not invertible.

## Conclusion

A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero.

- Matrix \(A\) is invertible because \(\det(A)=22\neq0\).
- Matrix \(B\) is not invertible because \(\det(B)=0\).

Therefore, **only the first matrix is invertible.**
