### Exercise 4. Matrix Multiplication and Noncommutativity

Given:

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
B=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\3&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

**1. Calculate AB:**

$$
AB=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\3&1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}8&2\\3&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

**2. Calculate BA:**

$$
BA=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\3&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\3&7\end{pmatrix}
$$

**3. Is AB = BA?**

No, because

$$
\begin{pmatrix}8&2\\3&1\end{pmatrix}
\ne
\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\3&7\end{pmatrix}
$$

**4. Explanation:**

Matrix multiplication is noncommutative because changing the order of the matrices usually changes the result. In this example, AB is not equal to BA.
