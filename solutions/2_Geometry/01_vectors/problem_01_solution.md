# Exercise 1: Vector Operations and Their Meaning

Given the vectors

\[
u=(2,-1,3), \qquad v=(-1,4,2).
\]

## 1. Vector Addition: \(u+v\)

Add the corresponding components of the vectors.

| Component | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| \(x\) | \(2+(-1)\) | \(1\) |
| \(y\) | \(-1+4\) | \(3\) |
| \(z\) | \(3+2\) | \(5\) |

Therefore,

\[
u+v=(2+(-1),-1+4,3+2)
\]

\[
\boxed{u+v=(1,3,5)}
\]

## 2. Vector Subtraction: \(u-v\)

Subtract the corresponding components.

| Component | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| \(x\) | \(2-(-1)\) | \(3\) |
| \(y\) | \(-1-4\) | \(-5\) |
| \(z\) | \(3-2\) | \(1\) |

Therefore,

\[
u-v=(2-(-1),-1-4,3-2)
\]

\[
\boxed{u-v=(3,-5,1)}
\]

## 3. Scalar Operations: \(3u-2v\)

First, multiply each component by its scalar.

\[
3u=3(2,-1,3)=(6,-3,9)
\]

\[
2v=2(-1,4,2)=(-2,8,4)
\]

Now subtract the resulting vectors.

| Component | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| \(x\) | \(6-(-2)\) | \(8\) |
| \(y\) | \(-3-8\) | \(-11\) |
| \(z\) | \(9-4\) | \(5\) |

Therefore,

\[
3u-2v=(6-(-2),-3-8,9-4)
\]

\[
\boxed{3u-2v=(8,-11,5)}
\]

## 4. Verify that \((u+v)-v=u\)

From Part 1,

\[
u+v=(1,3,5).
\]

Subtract \(v=(-1,4,2)\):

\[
(u+v)-v=(1,3,5)-(-1,4,2)
\]

\[
=(1-(-1),3-4,5-2)
\]

\[
=(2, -1, 3).
\]

Since \(u=(2,-1,3)\),

\[
\boxed{(u+v)-v=u}.
\]

The identity is verified.

## 5. Geometric Meaning of Vector Addition

Vector addition represents the combination of two vectors, including both their directions and magnitudes.

Using the **head-to-tail method**, place the tail of vector \(v\) at the head of vector \(u\). The resultant vector \(u+v\) extends from the tail of \(u\) to the head of the translated \(v\).

For the given vectors,

\[
u=(2,-1,3), \qquad v=(-1,4,2),
\]

their sum is

\[
u+v=(1,3,5).
\]

This means that the combined displacement is \(1\) unit in the \(x\)-direction, \(3\) units in the \(y\)-direction, and \(5\) units in the \(z\)-direction.

Alternatively, when both vectors start at the same point, they form adjacent sides of a parallelogram. The diagonal from their common starting point represents the resultant vector \(u+v\).
