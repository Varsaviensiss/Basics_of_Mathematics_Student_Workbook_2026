# Exercise 1: Vector Operations and Their Meaning

Given the vectors

\[
u=(2,-1,3), \qquad v=(-1,4,2).
\]

## 1. Compute \(u+v\)

Add the corresponding components of the vectors:

\[
u+v=(2,-1,3)+(-1,4,2).
\]

\[
u+v=(2+(-1),-1+4,3+2).
\]

Simplifying,

\[
\boxed{u+v=(1,3,5)}.
\]

## 2. Compute \(u-v\)

Subtract the corresponding components:

\[
u-v=(2,-1,3)-(-1,4,2).
\]

\[
u-v=(2-(-1),-1-4,3-2).
\]

Simplifying,

\[
\boxed{u-v=(3,-5,1)}.
\]

## 3. Compute \(3u-2v\)

First, multiply each vector by its scalar:

\[
3u=3(2,-1,3)=(6,-3,9),
\]

\[
2v=2(-1,4,2)=(-2,8,4).
\]

Now subtract the vectors:

\[
3u-2v=(6,-3,9)-(-2,8,4).
\]

\[
3u-2v=(6-(-2),-3-8,9-4).
\]

Simplifying,

\[
\boxed{3u-2v=(8,-11,5)}.
\]

## 4. Verify that \((u+v)-v=u\)

From Part 1,

\[
u+v=(1,3,5).
\]

Subtract \(v=(-1,4,2)\):

\[
(u+v)-v=(1,3,5)-(-1,4,2).
\]

\[
(u+v)-v=(1-(-1),3-4,5-2).
\]

Therefore,

\[
(u+v)-v=(2,-1,3).
\]

Since \(u=(2,-1,3)\),

\[
\boxed{(u+v)-v=u}.
\]

Thus, the identity is verified.

## 5. Geometric Meaning of Vector Addition

Geometrically, adding two vectors means combining their directions and magnitudes according to the vector addition rule.

To add \(u\) and \(v\), place the tail of \(v\) at the head of \(u\). The resultant vector \(u+v\) starts at the tail of \(u\) and ends at the head of the translated vector \(v\). This is called the **head-to-tail rule**.

Alternatively, if both vectors start at the same point, they form two adjacent sides of a parallelogram, and the diagonal from their common starting point represents their sum.

For the given vectors,

\[
u+v=(1,3,5).
\]

Therefore, the resultant vector has components \((1,3,5)\), representing the combined displacement of \(u\) and \(v\).
