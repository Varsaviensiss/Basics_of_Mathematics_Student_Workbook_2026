# Exercise 4. Dot Product and Perpendicularity

## Given

$$
\mathbf{u}=(1,2,-1)
$$

$$
\mathbf{v}=(2,-1,3)
$$

## 1. Compute the Dot Product \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}\)

The dot product of two three-dimensional vectors is

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3
$$

Substitute the components:

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}
=1(2)+2(-1)+(-1)(3)
$$

$$
=2-2-3
$$

$$
=-3
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=-3}
$$

## 2. Determine Whether the Vectors Are Perpendicular

Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular if and only if their dot product equals zero:

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0
$$

However, we obtained

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=-3\neq0
$$

Therefore, the vectors are not perpendicular.

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{u}\text{ and }\mathbf{v}\text{ are not perpendicular}}
$$

## 3. Find \(a\) So That \(\mathbf{w}=(2,-1,a)\) Is Perpendicular to \(\mathbf{u}\)

For perpendicular vectors, the dot product must be zero:

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
$$

Substitute \(\mathbf{u}=(1,2,-1)\) and \(\mathbf{w}=(2,-1,a)\):

$$
1(2)+2(-1)+(-1)(a)=0
$$

Simplify:

$$
2-2-a=0
$$

$$
-a=0
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{a=0}
$$

The resulting vector is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{w}=(2,-1,0)}
$$

## 4. Check the Result

Calculate the dot product using \(a=0\):

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}
=(1,2,-1)\cdot(2,-1,0)
$$

$$
=1(2)+2(-1)+(-1)(0)
$$

$$
=2-2+0
$$

$$
=0
$$

Since the dot product is zero, the vectors are perpendicular.

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0}
$$

Thus, the result is verified.

## Final Answers

- **Dot product:** \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=-3\)
- **Are \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) perpendicular?** No.
- **Required value:** \(a=0\)
- **Resulting vector:** \(\mathbf{w}=(2,-1,0)\)
- **Verification:** \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0\), so the vectors are perpendicular.
