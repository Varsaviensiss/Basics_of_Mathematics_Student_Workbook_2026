## Exercise 1. Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form

### 1. Circle

**Equation:**

\[
x^2+y^2=9
\]

- **Type:** Circle
- **Center:** \(C=(0,0)\)
- **Radius:** \(r=3\)
- **Intercepts:** \((\pm3,0)\), \((0,\pm3)\)

**Explanation:** Both variables are squared with equal positive coefficients. Therefore, the curve is a circle centered at the origin.

**Sketch:** A circle centered at \((0,0)\) with radius \(3\).

### 2. Ellipse

**Equation:**

\[
\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1
\]

- **Type:** Ellipse
- **Center:** \(C=(0,0)\)
- **Semiaxes:** \(a=4,\ b=3\)
- **Intercepts:** \((\pm4,0)\), \((0,\pm3)\)

**Explanation:** Both squared terms are positive, but their denominators differ. Since \(16>9\), the ellipse is stretched along the \(x\)-axis.

**Sketch:** An ellipse centered at the origin, wider horizontally than vertically.

### 3. Parabola

**Equation:**

\[
y^2=8x
\]

- **Type:** Parabola
- **Vertex:** \(V=(0,0)\)
- **Axis of symmetry:** \(y=0\)
- **Opening direction:** Positive \(x\)-direction

Comparing with the standard form \(y^2=4px\):

\[
4p=8\implies p=2
\]

**Explanation:** Only one variable is squared, so the equation represents a parabola.

**Sketch:** A parabola with vertex at the origin opening to the right.

### 4. Hyperbola

**Equation:**

\[
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1
\]

- **Type:** Hyperbola
- **Center:** \(C=(0,0)\)
- **Semiaxes:** \(a=3,\ b=2\)
- **Vertices:** \((\pm3,0)\)
- **Asymptotes:**

\[
y=\pm\frac{b}{a}x
=\pm\frac{2}{3}x
\]

**Explanation:** The squared terms have opposite signs, which identifies a hyperbola. Since the positive term is \(x^2/9\), its branches open to the left and right.

**Sketch:** Two branches opening left and right, approaching the asymptotes \(y=\pm\frac23x\).
