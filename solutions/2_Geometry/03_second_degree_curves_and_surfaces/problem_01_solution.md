Exercise 1. Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form

1(a) (x^2+y^2=9)

This is a circle.

Center: ((0,0))

Radius: (r=3)

Intercepts: ((3,0),(-3,0),(0,3),(0,-3))

The standard equation of a circle is (x^2+y^2=r^2). Since (r^2=9), the radius is (3).

Sketch: Draw a circle centered at the origin with radius (3).

1(b) (\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1)

This is an ellipse.

Center: ((0,0))

Horizontal semiaxis: (a=4)

Vertical semiaxis: (b=3)

Vertices: ((\pm4,0)), ((0,\pm3))

The denominator under (x^2) is larger, so the ellipse is stretched horizontally.

Sketch: Draw a horizontal ellipse centered at the origin.

1(c) (y^2=8x)

This is a parabola.

Compare it with the standard form (y^2=4px):

[
4p=8\Rightarrow p=2
]

Vertex: ((0,0))

Axis of symmetry: the (x)-axis

Opening direction: to the right

Sketch: Draw a parabola with its vertex at the origin, opening to the right.

1(d) (\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1)

This is a hyperbola.

Center: ((0,0))

Vertices: ((\pm3,0))

Asymptotes:

[
y=\pm\frac{2}{3}x
]

Since the (x^2) term is positive and comes first, the hyperbola opens to the left and right.

Sketch: Draw two branches opening in opposite horizontal directions and approaching the asymptotes.

Conclusion

The equations can be recognized by their canonical forms:

(x^2+y^2=r^2): Circle

(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1): Ellipse

(y^2=4px): Parabola

(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1): Hyperbola
