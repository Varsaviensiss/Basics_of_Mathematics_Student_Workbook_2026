## Exercise 6. Shifted Ellipse

### Given Equation

\[
4x^2+9y^2-16x+18y-11=0
\]

### 1. Reduce to Canonical Form

Group the terms and complete the square:

\[
4(x^2-4x)+9(y^2+2y)=11
\]

\[
4[(x-2)^2-4]+9[(y+1)^2-1]=11
\]

Expand and simplify:

\[
4(x-2)^2-16+9(y+1)^2-9=11
\]

\[
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2=36
\]

Divide both sides by \(36\):

\[
\boxed{\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1}
\]

### 2. Find the Center and Semiaxis Lengths

Compare with the standard ellipse equation:

\[
\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
\]

Therefore:

- **Center:** \(C=(h,k)=(2,-1)\)
- **Horizontal semiaxis:** \(a=\sqrt{9}=3\)
- **Vertical semiaxis:** \(b=\sqrt{4}=2\)

Since \(a>b\), the major axis is horizontal.

### 3. Verify the Center

Substitute \(x=2\) and \(y=-1\) into the original left-hand side:

\[
4(2)^2+9(-1)^2-16(2)+18(-1)-11
\]

\[
=16+9-32-18-11
\]

\[
\boxed{-36}
\]

This agrees with the completed-square form:

\[
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2=36
\]

At the center, \(x-2=0\) and \(y+1=0\). Thus, the original left-hand side equals \(-36\), confirming the result.

### Final Answer

\[
\boxed{\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1}
\]

- **Type:** Ellipse
- **Center:** \((2,-1)\)
- **Semiaxis lengths:** \(3\) and \(2\)
- **Major axis:** Horizontal
