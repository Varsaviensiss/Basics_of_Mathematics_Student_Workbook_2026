
## Exercise 6. Shifted Ellipse

**Given equation:**

\[
4x^2+9y^2-16x+18y-11=0
\]

### 1. Complete the Square

Group the terms:

\[
4(x^2-4x)+9(y^2+2y)-11=0
\]

Complete the square:

\[
4[(x-2)^2-4]+9[(y+1)^2-1]-11=0
\]

Expand and simplify:

\[
4(x-2)^2-16+9(y+1)^2-9-11=0
\]

\[
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2=36
\]

Divide both sides by \(36\):

\[
\boxed{\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1}
\]

### 2. Center and Semiaxis Lengths

Comparing with the standard ellipse equation

\[
\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1,
\]

we obtain:

- **Center:** \(C=(2,-1)\)
- **Horizontal semiaxis:** \(a=\sqrt{9}=3\)
- **Vertical semiaxis:** \(b=\sqrt{4}=2\)

The ellipse is stretched along the horizontal axis.

### 3. Check the Center

Substitute \(x=2\) and \(y=-1\) into the original left-hand side:

\[
4(2)^2+9(-1)^2-16(2)+18(-1)-11
\]

\[
=16+9-32-18-11
\]

\[
\boxed{-36}
\]

This is consistent with the completed-square equation:

\[
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2=36.
\]

At the center, both squared terms equal zero, so the original left-hand side equals \(-36\), as expected.

### Final Answer

\[
\boxed{\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1}
\]

- **Type:** Ellipse
- **Center:** \((2,-1)\)
- **Semiaxis lengths:** \(3\) and \(2\)
- **Major axis:** Horizontal
