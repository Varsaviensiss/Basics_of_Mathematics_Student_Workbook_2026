
# Problem 1. Derivative of a Polynomial and the Slope of a Tangent Line

Given the function

\[
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4,
\]

find \(f'(x)\), calculate \(f'(0)\) and \(f'(1)\), determine the signs of these values, and explain what they tell us about the slopes of the tangent lines.

## Step 1: Find the Derivative

Use the power rule:

\[
\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}.
\]

Differentiate each term separately:

\[
\frac{d}{dx}(3x^5)=15x^4,
\]

\[
\frac{d}{dx}(-2x^3)=-6x^2,
\]

\[
\frac{d}{dx}(7x)=7,
\]

\[
\frac{d}{dx}(-4)=0.
\]

Combining these results gives

\[
\boxed{f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7}.
\]

## Step 2: Calculate \(f'(0)\)

Substitute \(x=0\) into the derivative:

\[
f'(0)=15(0)^4-6(0)^2+7.
\]

Simplifying,

\[
f'(0)=7.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{f'(0)=7}.
\]

Since \(7>0\), the derivative is positive at \(x=0\). The tangent line has a positive slope, so the graph is increasing at this point.

## Step 3: Calculate \(f'(1)\)

Substitute \(x=1\):

\[
f'(1)=15(1)^4-6(1)^2+7.
\]

Simplifying,

\[
f'(1)=15-6+7.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{f'(1)=16}.
\]

Since \(16>0\), the derivative is also positive at \(x=1\). The tangent line has a positive slope, so the graph is increasing at this point as well.

## Step 4: Interpret the Results Geometrically

The derivative at a point represents the slope of the tangent line to the graph at that point.

- At \(x=0\), the slope is \(f'(0)=7\). The tangent line rises from left to right.
- At \(x=1\), the slope is \(f'(1)=16\). The tangent line also rises from left to right and is steeper than the tangent at \(x=0\).

The corresponding points on the graph are found using the original function.

At \(x=0\):

\[
f(0)=3(0)^5-2(0)^3+7(0)-4=-4.
\]

The point is \((0,-4)\).

At \(x=1\):

\[
f(1)=3-2+7-4=4.
\]

The point is \((1,4)\).

## Final Answers

**Derivative:**

\[
\boxed{f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7}
\]

**At \(x=0\):**

\[
\boxed{f'(0)=7>0}
\]

The tangent line has a positive slope of \(7\).

**At \(x=1\):**

\[
\boxed{f'(1)=16>0}
\]

The tangent line has a positive slope of \(16\), making it steeper than the tangent at \(x=0\).

**Key idea:** A positive derivative means the tangent line slopes upward from left to right. A larger positive derivative means a steeper upward slope.
